updating my resume in google docs has always been annoying. every time i add a new experience or change something, i somehow end up fixing the formatting again. sometimes it's the spacing, sometimes the layout just doesn't look right.

i wanted something where i could just edit the content without having to worry about all that.

a while ago, i saw people online using typst to write their university final year reports. some universities even have their own templates for it, which got me interested.

i knew LaTeX could do something similar, but typst looked easier to learn. i also discussed it with AI to figure out which one would suit my needs better, and ended up choosing typst.

so i thought, why not try making my resume with it?

## building the template

i started building the template from scratch with the help of AI and the typst documentation.

the idea was simple. i wanted a clean resume that followed common HR recommendations and was designed to be ATS-friendly. nothing fancy, just a readable layout with all the necessary information.

i made reusable sections for education, work experience, projects, certifications, and skills. that way, whenever i need to update something, i can just change the content without manually adjusting the layout.

and that's probably what i like most about typst. once the template is set up, adding new things is really quick. i can edit the file, compile it, and get an updated PDF.

i also found that typst gives you quite a lot of freedom with document design. you can make something much more creative if you want, but for my resume i decided to keep it simple.

## sharing it

i figured other people might be dealing with the same formatting problems. so i decided to make it open source.

the repo is called [typst-resume](https://github.com/Mgkusumaputra/typst-resume). you can use it as a starting point for your own resume, customize the sections, or change the design entirely.

of course, using typst isn't necessary for everyone. if you're already comfortable with google docs, that's fine. but if you prefer working with code and want more control over your documents, i think it's worth trying.

for me, it solved a pretty simple problem. i can now update my resume without having to fix the formatting every time.
